1 Paystack live key Blocks all payments

The store currently runs on a Paystack test key. Checkout appears to work end to end, but no real money moves. To open for business we need your live secret key.

What to do

  • Complete Paystack’s business verification. They will ask for your CAC documents, a valid ID, and the bank account you want your settlements paid into. Live keys only appear once this is approved, so start here if you haven’t.
  • In the Paystack dashboard, open Settings → API Keys & Webhooks and copy the Live Secret Key. It begins with sk_live_.
  • Send it to us in a direct message only — never in a group chat or a shared email thread — and delete your copy of the message afterwards. The key lives on our server and is never included in the website or the app.
  • On that same page a webhook URL has to be saved. We’ll send you the exact line to paste, or we can do it ourselves if you give us dashboard access for a few minutes.
Why it matters: this is the one item that stands between the shop and its first real sale. The webhook half matters too — without it, a customer who pays and then closes their browser has their money taken while their order sits unconfirmed.

2 Domain and web address

You’ve bought the domain already, which is the hard part. We now need to point it at the finished store. Today the site can only be reached at a temporary address ending in .vercel.app.

What to do

  • Confirm the exact domain name you registered.
  • Either give us access to the account where you bought it, or add the handful of DNS records we’ll send you. Access is far quicker; a short screen-share also works if you’d prefer to keep the login.
Why it matters: it’s the address customers will type and trust. It also makes your shop links show a proper picture and title when shared on WhatsApp or Facebook instead of appearing blank — and your business email in the next item depends on this same domain.

3 Business email on your domain

The store needs to send email: order receipts, and the confirmation link people click when they create an account. These have to come from your own domain. Customers also need a real address to reply to.

What to do

  • We recommend Zoho Mail. It gives you proper addresses on your domain, such as info@abdmall.com and orders@abdmall.com, and its free plan is more than enough to begin with.
  • Create the Zoho account using the domain from item 2, then tell us — we’ll connect it to the store and test it.
If this sounds like a hassle, don’t do it. Tell us and Sodiq will set the whole thing up on your behalf. All we need is the domain access from item 2, and you’ll be handed the finished mailbox.
Why it matters: until this is in place, new customers cannot confirm their accounts and nobody receives a receipt. Email sent from an unverified domain is also treated as spam by Gmail and Yahoo, so it quietly never arrives.

4 Apple and Google developer accounts

The mobile app is built for both iPhone and Android. Only Apple and Google can publish it, and each requires a paid developer account. These must be registered in your business’s name, not ours. They are the legal publisher of your app, and you should own them permanently — if they sat with us, you could not move the app elsewhere later.

Apple Developer Program — $99 per year

  • Required to publish on the App Store, and also to install the app on any real iPhone for testing. There is no free route on iPhone.
  • If you enrol as a company rather than as an individual, Apple asks for a D-U-N-S number for your business. It’s free to request but can take several days, so it’s worth starting this one early.

Google Play Developer — $25, paid once

  • Register as a business or organisation, not as a personal account. Google currently makes brand-new personal accounts run a closed test with a group of testers for two weeks before the app may be released publicly; registering as a business avoids that wait. Worth confirming as you sign up, since Google changes these rules from time to time.

Then

  • Invite us to both accounts as a developer, and we’ll upload the builds and manage the submissions.
Why it matters: the app is finished, but it cannot reach a single customer’s phone without these. Android review typically takes a day or two; Apple’s first review is usually slower.

A few small extras

These are quick, and we only need them once the items above are in motion:

  • A privacy policy and terms page. Both app stores reject apps without one. We’ll write it — we just need your registered business name, address and a support email to put in it.
  • A support phone number and email for the website footer and the app store listings.
  • The bank account details Paystack should settle your takings into, if you haven’t already given them during verification.

What happens once we have them

Items 1 to 3 can all be done in a single afternoon, and the web store can start taking live payments the same day they arrive. The mobile apps follow as soon as the developer accounts exist; we’ll handle the builds, the store listings and the submissions from there.

If anything above is unclear, or you’d simply rather we did it, just ask.
